\title{Data Selection and Training Budget for Reasoning\\Supervised Fine-Tuning of Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Hanze Li, Sijie Chen, Yuan Zhou, and Yiming Liang}
\IEEEauthorblockA{Group 2, Trustworthy Machine Learning (Fall 2026)\\
Virginia Tech, Blacksburg, VA 24061 USA}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We study training-data selection for supervised fine-tuning (SFT) of Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct on long chain-of-thought (CoT)
demonstrations. From a 100K subset of AceReason-1.1-SFT, we select 15K examples by domain filtering, exact deduplication,
length filtering to the training context, and stratified sampling over source and length, and compare against a uniformly
random 15K subset. On eight math and science benchmarks under the official evaluation protocol, our final model, trained for
five epochs on the selected set, reaches a macro \passk{8} of 0.590 and \passk{1} of 0.269 over the six leaderboard
benchmarks, versus 0.539 and 0.262 for the base model and 0.549 and 0.246 for the random baseline. The 95\% confidence
intervals of a paired bootstrap exclude zero for the \passk{8} gain over the base model and for the \passk{1} gain over the
random baseline. The improvement comes mainly from fewer generations truncated at the 32,768-token limit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The project~\cite{course} fine-tunes Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct~\cite{qwen25} on 15,000 examples drawn from a 100K subset of
AceReason-1.1-SFT~\cite{acereason11}, whose responses are long CoT traces generated by DeepSeek-R1, and asks whether a
principled selection of the 15K examples outperforms random selection. Full-parameter SFT on such traces teaches the
student to imitate step-by-step reasoning, self-verification, and the boxed-answer format, but a 3B model reproduces long
traces imperfectly~\cite{li2025small}.

We construct a selected training set with a label-free pipeline that needs no model inference (Section~\ref{sec:data}),
train on it with the same recipe as the random baseline, and continue training for two additional epochs. The final model
improves macro \passk{8} by 0.051 over the base model and \passk{1} by 0.023 over the random baseline, and we trace both gains
to a lower truncation rate of the generated reasoning (Section~\ref{sec:results}).

\section{Data Selection Strategy}\label{sec:data}

The training pool contains 100,000 examples, of which 67,432 are math and 32,568 are code. The random baseline samples 15,000
examples uniformly with a fixed seed and no filtering; 556 of them exceed the 16,384-token training context and are
truncated, so their final answers are removed from the training target.

Our selected set applies four steps in order. First, \emph{domain filtering} keeps only math examples, since all target
benchmarks are math or science and code traces are off-target. Second, \emph{exact deduplication} removes repeated prompts
(7,730 among the math examples) and keeps the first occurrence, which increases prompt diversity~\cite{acereason11}. Third,
\emph{length filtering} keeps only examples whose full conversation fits the 16,384-token context under the Qwen chat
template (removing 2,292), so that every training target ends with its final answer and end-of-turn token. Fourth,
\emph{stratified sampling} with proportional allocation draws 15,000 of the 57,410 remaining examples over strata defined by
data source (NuminaMath-CoT, OpenMathReasoning) and total-length band (up to 4K, 4--8K, and 8--16K tokens), which preserves the
source mix and the difficulty spectrum implied by solution length. The selected set has 107.6M training tokens per epoch
(113.4M for random), a median of 6.5K tokens per example, and 2,252 examples in common with the random set.

The design follows evidence that a small, curated fine-tuning set can match or exceed a larger unfiltered one for
alignment~\cite{lima} and reasoning~\cite{s1,limo}, and the question filtering and source mixing studied for reasoning data
recipes~\cite{openthoughts}. A data-contamination check found six pool prompts that match a benchmark question verbatim; one
falls into each 15K set and is kept and disclosed rather than removed from only one arm.

\section{Experimental Setup}

\subsection{Training}
Both models are trained by full-parameter SFT with LLaMA-Factory~\cite{llamafactory}, using DeepSpeed ZeRO-3~\cite{zero},
FlashAttention-2~\cite{flashattn2}, and the Liger fused linear cross-entropy kernel~\cite{liger}, with the loss computed on
response tokens only. Table~\ref{tab:hparams} lists the hyperparameters. The final model is trained for three epochs and then
resumed from the epoch-3 weights, optimizer, and scheduler states for epochs 4 and 5 on a five-epoch cosine schedule.

\subsection{Evaluation}
We evaluate on AIME24, MATH-500, CN-Math 2024, Kaoyan, AMC, Minerva, OlympiadBench, and GPQA with the course harness, which
generates with vLLM~\cite{vllm} and grades the last boxed answer by symbolic equivalence. Each question receives $n=8$
samples at temperature 0.6, top-$p$ 0.95, and a maximum of 32,768 generated tokens. \passk{8} counts a question as solved if
any of the eight samples is correct, the \passk{k} metric of~\cite{codex} with $k=n$, and \passk{1} is the mean per-sample
accuracy. The macro score averages the six leaderboard benchmarks available to us (CN-Math 2024, Kaoyan, AMC, Minerva,
OlympiadBench, and GPQA) with equal weight; AIME24 and MATH-500 are reported but excluded from the leaderboard average.
Differences between models are reported with 95\% confidence intervals from a paired bootstrap with $B=2000$ that resamples
questions and keeps the eight samples of a question together~\cite{efron1993,miller2024}.

Table~\ref{tab:results} reports all eight benchmarks. Fine-tuning on the random subset changes the macro \passk{8} of the base
model by only $+0.011$ $[-0.042, +0.062]$ and lowers its \passk{1} by $0.016$ $[-0.036, +0.004]$. The final model improves macro
\passk{8} over the base model by $+0.051$ $[+0.009, +0.095]$, the only gain over the base model whose interval excludes zero,
and over the random baseline by $+0.040$ $[-0.004, +0.086]$ in \passk{8} and $+0.023$ $[+0.009, +0.038]$ in \passk{1}. Per
benchmark, the largest \passk{8} gains are on Kaoyan ($+0.131$ $[+0.070, +0.191]$ over the base model) and AMC ($+0.200$
$[+0.050, +0.375]$ over the random baseline), and the clearest \passk{1} gain over the random baseline is on OlympiadBench
($+0.023$ $[+0.012, +0.035]$). On AIME24, which is not in the macro average, the final model is below the random baseline
($-0.100$ $[-0.200, 0.000]$).

\emph{Truncation is the main source of error.} Both fine-tuned models generate CoT traces 8--16 times longer than the base
model, consistent with the overthinking of long-reasoning models~\cite{chen2024overthinking}. As shown in
Fig.~\ref{fig:cutoff}, 8.7--26.5\% of the random baseline's samples on the leaderboard benchmarks reach the 32,768-token limit
before giving an answer, and a truncated sample is almost always graded wrong (accuracy 0--4\%). The final model lowers the
truncation rate on every leaderboard benchmark, to 4.8--16.7\%, while the accuracy of completed samples stays comparable
(e.g., 0.355 versus 0.352 on OlympiadBench and 0.309 versus 0.296 on Minerva). Fewer truncations therefore translate directly
into higher \passk{1} and \passk{8}. Two factors are likely to contribute: length filtering ensures that every training target
is a complete solution with an answer, whereas the random set contains 556 truncated targets, and longer training continues to
reduce run-on generation. On a held-out set of 200 pool questions in neither training set, the truncation rate of the
selected run falls from 55\% after epoch 1 to 24\% after epoch 3 and 16\% after epoch 5, in line with the finding that SFT
accuracy on this data family improves up to about five epochs~\cite{acereason11}.

\emph{Data selection versus training budget.} The final model is trained for five epochs and the random baseline for three,
so their difference combines data selection and training budget. At an equal budget of three epochs, the selected set reaches
0.566 and 0.244, a \passk{8} gain of $+0.016$ $[-0.024, +0.056]$ over the random baseline that lies within question-sampling
noise. The gain of the final model is therefore best attributed to the selected data and the longer training together.

\emph{Limitations.} Each configuration is trained once, so the intervals cover question sampling but not training-seed
variance. The five-epoch schedule was chosen after the three-epoch results were known, and the final model is selected on the
benchmarks it is reported on; AIME25, which is on the course leaderboard but not available to us, is the only unseen test.
All training data come from the provided pool. Adding external data is allowed but would require decontamination; short,
curated reasoning sets~\cite{s1,limo}, which suit small students~\cite{li2025small}, and science data for GPQA are natural
extensions. Additional selection methods we evaluated, including a learning-progress selector, are reported in the full
report in the repository.

\section{Conclusion}

A label-free selection of complete, deduplicated math demonstrations, combined with five epochs of SFT, raises the macro
\passk{8} of Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct from 0.539 to 0.590 and its \passk{1} from 0.262 to 0.269, and outperforms the random
baseline (0.549 and 0.246), mainly by reducing truncated generations. Code, configurations, and evaluation logs are available
at \url{https://github.com/yuan-zhou-git/Course_Project_Trustworthy_ML}; the final model and datasets are available at
\url{https://huggingface.co/yuan-zhou-hf}.
